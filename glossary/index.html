Glossary

Terms used throughout this manual, defined once here rather than re-explained on every page.

Aerogel
An extremely low-density, low-thermal-conductivity solid used for spacecraft and rover insulation. Flight-proven on Mars Exploration Rover missions.
Conjunction (solar conjunction)
The period when Mars passes nearly behind the Sun as seen from Earth, blocking reliable radio communication for roughly two weeks, approximately every 26 months.
ECLSS
Environmental Control and Life Support System — the general term for the equipment that manages breathable air, water recycling, and waste processing in a crewed spacecraft or habitat. The ISS's ECLSS is the primary operational precedent for Mars habitat design.
EVA
Extravehicular activity: any operation conducted outside a pressurized vehicle or habitat, in a pressure suit.
GCR (galactic cosmic ray)
High-energy particle radiation originating outside the solar system. A continuous background radiation source that Mars's thin atmosphere and absent magnetic field do little to block.
ISRU
In-situ resource utilization: producing usable resources (oxygen, water, propellant, construction material) directly from local Martian materials rather than transporting them from Earth.
MSD (Mars Sol Date)
A continuous day-count for Mars, analogous to the Julian Date on Earth, used to track elapsed sols independent of any specific mission's local calendar. Shown live in the telemetry strip above.
MTC (Coordinated Mars Time)
A planet-wide reference time for Mars, analogous to Coordinated Universal Time on Earth, expressed on a 24-hour clock scaled to the Martian sol. Shown live in the telemetry strip above.
Perchlorate
A class of oxidizing salt present in Martian regolith at roughly 0.5–1% concentration by mass, toxic to humans and most food crops. Must be removed from soil and water before use.
Regolith
The loose, fragmented material — dust, soil, broken rock — covering the Martian bedrock. Distinct from Earth soil in that it contains no organic material and is not the product of biological weathering.
RHU (radioisotope heater unit)
A small device that provides steady heat from the natural decay of a radioisotope, independent of sunlight. Used on multiple Mars rover and lander missions to keep sensitive components within operating temperature.
Sol
One Martian solar day, equal to 24 hours 39 minutes 35 seconds — about 2.7% longer than an Earth day.
SPE (solar particle event)
A burst of high-energy particles from the Sun, typically associated with solar flares or coronal mass ejections. Unpredictable and capable of raising surface radiation dose sharply within hours.
Synodic period
The time between successive close alignments of Earth and Mars in their orbits, approximately 780 days (about 26 months). Governs both the timing of efficient transfer windows and the recurrence of solar conjunction.
τ (tau, dust opacity)
A measure of how much sunlight is blocked by suspended atmospheric dust; higher values indicate hazier skies and reduced solar power generation. Rises sharply during dust storms.

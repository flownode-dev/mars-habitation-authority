About this manual

The Mars Habitation Authority (MHA) publishes and maintains the reference standards for sustaining human life on the Martian surface: atmospheric management, water extraction, radiation shielding, thermal control, food production, gravitational health, communications, and habitat engineering. This manual consolidates those standards into a single public-facing field reference, consistent with the Authority's mandate to keep survival-critical information accurate, current, and freely available.

All figures on this site are drawn from established planetary science and mission engineering data. Where a figure is a typical or reference value rather than a live reading, it is labeled accordingly.

01–08

Life-support systems

Eight systems form the minimum baseline for sustained habitation. Numbering reflects operational priority, not publication order.

01 Atmosphere & Oxygen CO₂ management, in-situ O₂ production, breathable-air standards. 02 Water Extraction Polar ice, subsurface ice, hydrated regolith, closed-loop recycling. 03 Radiation Shielding Regolith mass shielding, water walls, lava-tube habitats. 04 Thermal Management Diurnal swings of up to 145°C; insulation and active heating. 05 Food Production Controlled-environment agriculture; perchlorate remediation of soil. 06 Gravity & Health 0.38 g physiological effects; exercise and countermeasure protocol. 07 Communications 3–22 minute one-way delay; conjunction blackout planning. 08 Habitat Pressure-vessel design, airlock procedure, structural standards.

Primary hazard notice

Perchlorate-contaminated regolith — do not ingest, inhale, or cultivate in untreated soil

Martian regolith contains perchlorate salts at approximately 0.5–1% concentration by mass, toxic to humans and most food crops. Soil must be processed to remove perchlorates before use in agriculture or exposure to crew. See the full hazard reference for handling procedure.

MHA-HAZ-001 · Consolidated Hazard Reference

Hazard Reference

Every hazard notice referenced across the eight life-support systems, consolidated in one place. Review this reference periodically, not only once.

01

Pressure & atmosphere

Rapid decompression

Any breach of a pressurized volume is a survival-critical emergency; hypoxia impairs judgment within seconds. All pressurized volumes require redundant breach detection and reachable emergency shelter. Related: Atmosphere & Oxygen.

02

Perchlorate exposure

Perchlorate-contaminated regolith, ice, and untreated water

Regolith across most of Mars contains perchlorate salts at roughly 0.5–1% concentration by mass, toxic to humans if ingested or inhaled, and damaging to most food crops. Never consume unprocessed local water or cultivate in unremediated soil. Related: Water Extraction, Food Production.

03

Radiation events

Solar particle events

Large solar particle events are unpredictable and can raise dose rates sharply within hours. Monitor space-weather alerts before and during EVA, and keep shielded shelter within rapid reach at all times. Related: Radiation Shielding.

04

Thermal & equipment

Cold-soak equipment failure

Unheated equipment left outside overnight can cold-soak below its operating range within hours, risking battery damage and material embrittlement. All surface equipment requires a heating plan before nightfall. Related: Thermal Management.

05

Post-transit deconditioning

Crew are physically compromised immediately after transit

Months in microgravity leave crew unfit for full EVA workload on arrival. Graduated reconditioning precedes unrestricted surface activity. Related: Gravity & Health, Your First 100 Sols.

06

Communications

No real-time Earth contact during blackout or delay

One-way delay runs 3–22 minutes at all times, and solar conjunction removes Earth contact entirely for roughly two weeks, about every 26 months. Emergencies during these windows must be handled with onsite resources and protocol alone. Related: Communications.

07

Dust

Dust ingress and seal degradation

Fine, abrasive, electrostatically clinging regolith dust degrades seals and equipment over time, and regional or global dust storms can suppress solar power generation for extended periods. Airlock dust-exclusion procedure and scheduled seal inspection are mandatory. Related: Habitat.

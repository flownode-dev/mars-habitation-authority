Radiation Shielding

Protection from galactic cosmic rays and solar particle events on a planet with no global magnetic field and a fraction of Earth's atmospheric shielding.

03.1

Why Mars offers little natural protection

Mars lost its global magnetic field roughly four billion years ago, and its atmosphere is under 1% the mass, per unit area, of Earth's. Together, Earth's magnetosphere and thick atmosphere absorb the great majority of incoming galactic cosmic rays (GCRs) and solar particle events (SPEs) before they reach the surface. Mars has neither defense at meaningful strength, so surface radiation dose rates are far higher than on Earth, and comparable in kind — though generally lower in rate — to what astronauts experience in interplanetary space.

03.2

Measured surface dose

NASA's Curiosity rover has carried the Radiation Assessment Detector (RAD) on the Martian surface since 2012, providing the primary direct measurement of surface dose. RAD's published results show an average surface dose rate on the order of 0.6–0.7 millisieverts per sol, extrapolating to roughly 200–250 millisieverts per year of galactic cosmic ray exposure, with additional spikes during solar particle events. For comparison, average background radiation exposure on Earth is on the order of a few millisieverts per year.

03.3

Shielding strategies

Mass shielding is the primary defense: roughly 2–3 meters of compacted regolith attenuates GCR dose to substantially safer levels, following the same principle used to shield nuclear reactors and spacecraft. Practical approaches include burying or berming habitat modules under native regolith, building double-walled structures with a regolith- or water-filled cavity ("water walls"), and using naturally shielded terrain. Orbital imagery has identified lava-tube skylights in several Martian volcanic regions, including around Arsia Mons and Pavonis Mons; these collapsed lava tubes offer tens of meters of natural rock overburden and are a leading candidate for long-duration shielded habitation.

Hazards

Solar particle events — unshielded surface exposure during a solar event can deliver a dangerous acute dose

Large solar particle events are unpredictable and can raise dose rates sharply within hours. EVA planning requires monitoring space-weather alerts and having shielded shelter within rapid reach at all times. See related systems: habitat structure and gravity & health.

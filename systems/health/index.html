Gravity & Health

Physiological effects of sustained 0.38 g partial gravity, and the exercise and monitoring protocol required to counter them.

06.1

What is known from microgravity

No human has yet spent extended time at Martian partial gravity, so the health baseline is extrapolated from decades of microgravity research aboard the ISS and its predecessor stations. In freefall (0 g), astronauts without countermeasures lose bone mineral density at roughly 1–1.5% per month, concentrated in weight-bearing bones, alongside muscle atrophy, cardiovascular deconditioning, and fluid shifts toward the head. A documented ISS finding, Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS), links long-duration microgravity to changes in eye structure and vision. Mars's 0.38 g is expected to produce effects of the same kind but reduced severity relative to 0 g, based on centrifuge and bed-rest analog studies, though the exact rate at partial gravity remains an open research question.

06.2

Countermeasures

The ISS protocol — roughly two hours of daily exercise combining resistive strength training and aerobic conditioning, using hardware such as the Advanced Resistive Exercise Device and a treadmill — is the baseline model for Mars crew health maintenance, adapted to partial-gravity biomechanics. Regular medical monitoring (bone density scans, cardiovascular assessment, vision testing) tracks individual response and adjusts exercise prescription accordingly. The six-to-nine-month interplanetary transit under microgravity that precedes Mars surface operations compounds the deconditioning risk, making the transition to partial-gravity surface life itself a monitored medical event.

Hazards

Deconditioning on arrival — crew are physically compromised immediately after transit, before surface adaptation

Crew arriving after months in microgravity transit are not immediately fit for full EVA workload. Graduated reconditioning protocol precedes unrestricted surface activity. See related systems: radiation shielding and communications.

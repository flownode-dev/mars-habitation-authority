MHA-ARR-001 · New-Arrival Orientation

Your First 100 Sols

A sol-by-sol orientation for crew arriving on the Martian surface after transit: medical reconditioning, first EVA, daily system checks, and emergency awareness.

01

Sols 1–7: reconditioning before EVA

After six to nine months of microgravity transit, crew are not immediately fit for full surface workload. The first week on the surface is a monitored reconditioning period: graduated exercise, cardiovascular and vestibular assessment, and bone/muscle status checks against pre-flight baselines. Unrestricted EVA participation is cleared individually, not as a group, once reconditioning benchmarks are met. See Gravity & Health for the physiological background.

02

Your first EVA

The first extravehicular activity follows standard airlock procedure: suit check, cabin depressurization in stages, hatch release, and a tethered or close-range excursion within immediate return distance of the airlock. Dust exclusion procedure on re-entry is mandatory — fine regolith dust must be removed from the suit exterior before the inner hatch opens. See Habitat for airlock procedure and Thermal Management for suit thermal limits before planning EVA duration and timing.

03

Daily life-support checklist

Every sol begins and ends with a systems check: habitat pressure and atmosphere composition, O₂ and CO₂ scrubber status, water recycling loop status, power and thermal margins, and dust-opacity conditions for planned EVA. None of this is automatic background infrastructure the way utilities are on Earth — every crew member is expected to read and understand the telemetry strip at the top of this manual and know what a departure from nominal values means.

04

Emergency awareness

Before your first EVA, know the location of the nearest shielded shelter from every point on your planned route, know the current space-weather alert status, and know that Earth contact carries a delay of minutes, not seconds — no emergency response from Earth arrives in real time. Consult the full hazard reference before working near any flagged hazard, and review it again periodically; it is not a one-time read.
